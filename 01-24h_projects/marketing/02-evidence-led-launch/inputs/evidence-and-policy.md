# Evidence packet and review policy

All sources are fictional. No outside sourcing is needed or authoritative.

**E1 — usability observation, exercise day -7.** Six operations coordinators each completed the same synthetic ownership-assignment task in a spreadsheet and then FenceNote. Median task time was 18 minutes in the spreadsheet and 12 minutes in FenceNote. Tool order was fixed, so practice may influence results. There was no statistical test, real deployment, complete triage workflow, measurement of remediation time, or proof of typical savings. Participants said owner visibility was helpful. Permission to publish anonymized aggregate observations is granted; no quotes are approved for attribution to real customers.

**E2 — performance note, exercise day -5.** One test file of 500 rows imported on a test laptop in 9 seconds. Other file sizes/devices, live service latency, uptime, and production behavior were not tested. This is a development observation, not a service guarantee.

**E3 — demo deck, version 1, exercise day -20.** “PDF export and ticket sync next release. Automatic duplicate merging in prototype.” This describes plans/experiments. R version 2 states what actually shipped and overrides the deck for released features. Roadmap dates and commitments are unapproved.

**E4 — draft sales note, exercise day -3.** “Everyone loved it; probably the fastest tool on the market. It should eliminate errors.” No comparison or error-rate study exists. This is opinion, not approved evidence.

**E5 — review policy, approved exercise day 0.** Use released product facts for capabilities and study-specific context for observed measurements. Do not generalize small synthetic tasks to customer outcomes, make comparative superiority claims without comparative data, invent certifications/pricing, or use “guaranteed” security outcomes. A qualified study observation may be used if its sample/task/limitations are visible and it is helpful to the audience; choosing to omit it is also acceptable. A CTA must say what is requested and avoid implying immediate access. Unknown or conflicting factual requests go to Inez for evidence and Mara for feature availability; Jules approves copy only after evidence decisions. Keep an auditable status such as approved, approved-with-qualification, held-for-evidence, or removed. Re-review when source version, audience, availability, or wording changes. You can design additional statuses if they map clearly to distribution permission.

**E6 — handoff constraint.** The team has time to maintain two launch assets, not a full channel campaign. They need approved copy and an actionable hold list with owners. No claims should be silently dropped from the review log, even if omitted from final copy. Never route fictional drafts to real people.
